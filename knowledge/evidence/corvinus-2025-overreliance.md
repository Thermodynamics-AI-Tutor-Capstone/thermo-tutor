# Corvinus 2025 — Decreasing knowledge and over-reliance

**Type:** study (randomized classroom experiment, **terminated early**)
**One line:** A university operations research class randomized into AI-permitted and
AI-prohibited cohorts found lower engagement and comprehension with unrestricted AI — and
the experiment was **forced to end early by student protests, media attention, and
government intervention.**
**Why we care:** Two things at once: a third independent negative result on unguarded AI,
and the only documented case of what happens when you randomize students out of AI access.
That second part is a direct risk to our own pilot design.

## The study

*"Impact of AI Tools on Learning Outcomes: Decreasing Knowledge and Over-Reliance"*
(arXiv:2510.16019). **Corvinus University of Budapest**, operations research class.

- Two randomly assigned cohorts: one **permitted** AI tools during classes and exams, one
  **prohibited**
- Point adjustments applied to equalize average performance between groups for fairness

**Finding:** *"uncontrolled use of AI tools leads to disengaged students and low
understanding of material."* Students with unrestricted access showed **lower engagement and
reduced comprehension** than peers without.

That is a third independent replication of the direction in
[Bastani](bastani-2025-harm.md) (−17% on unassisted exams) and consistent with
[Khanmigo's](khanmigo-engagement-2026.md) null.

## ⚠ The part that should change how we plan our pilot

Despite institutional approval, **student protests escalated to media attention and
government intervention**, and the experiment had to be substantially modified and
terminated before final exams. The authors' own reading: *"generative AI tools have already
become indispensable for students,"* as evidenced by the intensity of the backlash.

**Implications for us, and they are concrete:**

1. **Do not design a study that withholds AI from a control group.** Students now treat AI
   access as an entitlement, and in at least one documented case were willing to escalate to
   national media over it. Our [IRB](../../admin/irb.md) may approve such a design; the
   students may not accept it.
2. **This is a strong practical argument for [Kestin's](kestin-2025-rct.md) within-subject
   alternating design**, where every student gets the tool and serves as their own control.
   We had adopted it for statistical efficiency and research ethics. It is also the design
   least likely to trigger a revolt.
3. **The realistic comparison condition is not "no AI."** It is "the free frontier models
   students already have," which at Penn State means
   [AI Studio](../practice/psu-ai-landscape.md). Withholding is neither feasible nor
   enforceable. → [open question C4](../../docs/03-open-questions.md)
